02 — Your data

What actually happens to the data.

A factory reset is not deletion. It removes the pointer to the files, not the files — on modern phones the encryption key is what matters, and on a failing drive even a verified erase can leave fragments.

Cryptographic erase, then verify

Modern phones encrypt storage by default. Destroying the key makes the remaining ciphertext useless — fast, and the device survives. We run this with certified erasure software, check the completion log, then spot-check the device before it is listed for resale.

  • StandardNIST SP 800-88 Rev.2 — Purge
  • Also aligned toIEEE 2883-2022
  • ToolCertified erasure software
  • EvidenceErasure log + certificate per device

Physical destruction of the media

For devices with sensitive data, or storage that fails verification, the storage chip is destroyed rather than erased. This is the only basis on which “unrecoverable by any means” is an honest claim — and it is what the certificate will say.

  • MethodStorage media destroyed
  • ResultRecovery not possible
  • ChainCustody logged from collection
  • EvidenceDestruction certificate per device
An honest note

“100% deletion” is a marketing sentence, not a technical one. No erasure tool can prove a negative on failing flash memory. What we do instead is choose the method that fits the device, record which one was used, and put it on a certificate you can hold us to. You pick the method when you book: erase and resell, or destroy the storage.
